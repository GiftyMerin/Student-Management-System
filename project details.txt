1. Data Acquisition & Sources
You will need historical weather data coupled with actual solar generation data. Excellent free resources include:

PVGIS (Photovoltaic Geographical Information System): Offers free hourly solar radiation and PV generation data for almost any location globally.

Kaggle: Search for "solar power generation dataset" or "photovoltaic power prediction" for pre-cleaned CSV files containing weather features and power output targets.

NREL (National Renewable Energy Laboratory): Provides robust meteorological and solar datasets (like NSRDB).

2. Feature Engineering (The Secret Sauce)
Raw data rarely works straight out of the box. Create these predictive features to boost your model's accuracy:

Time-Based Features: Extract hour, day of the year, and month to help the model learn seasonal and daily sun patterns.

Lag Features: Include generation values from 1 hour or 24 hours ago, as current production is heavily correlated with recent history.

Interaction Features: Combine temperature and solar irradiance, since high heat often degrades panel efficiency despite high sunlight.

3. Model Training & Evaluation
Start Simple: Begin with a Random Forest Regressor or Ridge Regression to establish a baseline score.

Scale Up: Upgrade to gradient boosting algorithms like XGBoost or LightGBM, which excel at handling tabular weather data.

Metrics: Evaluate your model using RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) to penalize large errors heavily, and MAE (Mean Absolute Error) for an intuitive average error in kilowatts.
